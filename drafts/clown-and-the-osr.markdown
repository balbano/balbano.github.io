In [Principia Apocrypha](https://lithyscaphe.blogspot.com/p/principia-apocrypha.html), Ben Milton, Steven Lumpkin, and David Perry write: "Don't Mind The Fourth Wall: Don't worry much about 'metagaming', or the dissonance between what the players know and what the characters know. Favor the ingenuity of the players over strict personification of their characters."

In *The Moving Body*, Jacques Lecoq writes “The actor is masked, partly protected by the little red nose,” explaining how the clown is shielded from being hurt by the audience’s laughter. But he also writes, “You cannot play at being a clown; you are one… With the clown, I ask [the students] to be themselves as profoundly as they possibly can, and to observe the effect they produce on the world” (Lecoq, *The Moving Body*, 156, 159).

In a clown class, [Anne Zander](https://annezander.com/) said, "Your breath is my breath. Your heartbeat is my heartbeat," describing the relationship of clown to audience. 

"Your breath is my breath. Your heartbeat is my heartbeat."

That phrase has been stuck in my head ever since.

Clown plays without a 4th wall, and creates tension from the fact that the audience doesn't know the rules. In some ways, the idea to "not worry about metagaming" in OSR play creates a similar boundary-breaking tension.

Also, Triangle Agency!

from a discord comment about important games of the 2020s: 

I do think it's interesting to answer the question personally, rather than try to speculate on "what will be important generally" whatever that means.

Playing Triangle Agency was definitely important to me in terms of thinking about how you can create really interesting play experiences when you are intentionally blurring the boundary between player and character.

Like, if, within the fiction, "Brendan Albano" is the table-self of my character "Liv Butkiss", then does Liv knows everything I know, and what does that mean for ideas like "metagaming"? If I want my character to keep a secret from another character, do I the player want to keep the secret from the other player? Not to mention my obsession with the rule that "breaking the rules for narrative reasons earns your character a demerit".
